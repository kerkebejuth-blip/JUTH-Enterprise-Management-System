## 1. PURPOSE

This volume governs the use of AI-assisted development and future AI-assisted clinical capabilities within JUTH HOS.

## 2. AI ENGINEERING PHILOSOPHY

AI tools may accelerate analysis, documentation, testing, and implementation, but they do not define architecture.

All AI-assisted output remains subject to human review, architecture governance, security review, and clinical safety review where applicable.

## 3. DEVELOPMENT USE

AI-assisted development shall:

- Follow the Constitution
- Preserve existing architecture
- Avoid unapproved redesign
- Produce reviewable changes
- Document assumptions
- Run validation gates
- Avoid business feature implementation outside approved scope

## 4. ARCHITECTURAL CONTROL

AI prompts or outputs that change architecture, module boundaries, data models, security posture, or integration patterns require review before implementation.

## 5. CLINICAL AI PRINCIPLES

Future clinical AI capabilities shall be:

- Context-aware
- Auditable
- Non-blocking
- Explainable where practical
- Governed by clinical safety review
- Subject to professional responsibility

AI shall support healthcare professionals but shall not replace authorized clinical judgment.

## 6. DATA PROTECTION

AI workflows shall protect patient information, secrets, credentials, and institutional data.

Sensitive data shall not be exposed to unauthorized tools or external systems.

## 7. REVIEW AND VALIDATION

AI-generated changes require:

- Human review
- Architecture compliance review
- Security consideration
- Test validation
- Documentation review

## 8. SUMMARY

AI engineering governance ensures that AI strengthens JUTH HOS delivery while preserving architecture, safety, security, and accountability.
