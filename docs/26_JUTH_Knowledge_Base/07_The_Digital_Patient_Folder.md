# The Digital Patient Folder

## Institutional Principle

The digital patient folder is the conceptual centre of the JUTH Enterprise Hospital Operating System. The historical paper folder defines the logical workflow: one patient, one continuing record, many chapters, and a clear history of custody, documentation, and care.

## Lifelong Record

Every patient interaction contributes to one lifelong longitudinal record. The record includes identity and demographics, encounters, consultations, admissions, emergency care, clinical and nursing notes, vital signs, laboratory, radiology, procedures, theatre, medication orders, prescriptions, drug administration, referrals, discharge, follow-up, correspondence, attachments, scanned documents, consent evidence, billing references, and audit history.

Nothing becomes fragmented. Nothing becomes disconnected. Nothing becomes orphaned because it was generated by a different department or system. Each artifact retains its owning context, but it also carries the patient, encounter or service, provenance, lifecycle, and authorization relationships needed to reconstruct the complete story.

## Folder Index and Custody

Medical Records remains the custodian of the legal health record, folder index, record availability, filing, release, archive, retention, and physical or digital custody policy. Patient owns canonical identity and identity linkage. Clinical and operational contexts own the content and behavior of their records.

This separation prevents the digital folder from becoming a single ungoverned data store while preserving the continuity that the physical folder provides.

## Lifecycle

1. The patient is registered or resolved through the patient index.
2. The MRN or hospital number and other identifiers are linked with authority and provenance.
3. Each encounter, service movement, order, result, note, prescription, referral, procedure, and document is linked to the patient.
4. Medical Records and owning contexts maintain filing, signing, status, custody, and access history.
5. Corrections append versions rather than erasing evidence.
6. Merges and splits preserve source records, decisions, and downstream reconciliation.
7. Archive and restoration retain historical visibility for authorized use.
8. Downtime artifacts are reconciled and closed through controlled procedures.

## Readability Across Decades

The folder must remain interpretable for 20 to 30 years. Long-term readability requires stable non-semantic identifiers, human-readable MRN linkage, versioned contracts, explicit provenance, durable document metadata, migration discipline, backward-compatible APIs and events, and preservation of historical evidence.
