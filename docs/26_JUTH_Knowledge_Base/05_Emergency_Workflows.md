# Emergency Workflows

## Purpose

Emergency care requires rapid identity resolution, safe provisional registration, immediate clinical work, and later completion of the longitudinal record. Urgency must not create an invisible patient identity or a disconnected clinical history.

## Emergency Principles

- Clinical assessment and stabilization must not wait for a slow or unavailable downstream dependency when approved emergency policy permits safe provisional action.
- The patient must be linked to an existing identity when possible and assigned a controlled provisional identity when necessary.
- MRN or hospital-number uncertainty must remain visible and be reconciled by authorized staff.
- Emergency notes, observations, orders, results, procedures, medications, referrals, admissions, transfers, and discharge records must become part of the same patient timeline.
- Every emergency action must be attributable and auditable.

## Operational Flow

1. The emergency service captures the minimum identity and safety information required by policy.
2. The patient index is searched in parallel with immediate care where appropriate.
3. A current encounter and location are established, with provisional status clearly represented if identity is incomplete.
4. Clinical documentation, observations, investigations, procedures, medication orders, administration records, and referrals are linked to the patient and encounter.
5. When identity evidence is confirmed, the provisional record is reconciled without losing the emergency history.
6. Admission, transfer, referral, or discharge continues the same record and publishes the appropriate events.

## Safety and Reconciliation

The system must make provisional identity, duplicate risk, pending results, and outstanding reconciliation visible to authorized staff. No emergency record may be discarded because it was created before complete demographic verification. Recovery from downtime must include duplicate detection, MRN resolution, filing, and audit closure.
