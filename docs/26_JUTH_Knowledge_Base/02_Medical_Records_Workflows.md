# Medical Records Workflows

## Purpose

Medical Records is the institutional custodian of the legal health record and the operational discipline that keeps the patient folder available, complete, confidential, retrievable, and historically reliable.

## Core Responsibilities

Medical Records responsibilities include:

- Maintaining the patient index and registration record relationship.
- Assigning, retrieving, and protecting the MRN or hospital number according to approved policy.
- Assembling, indexing, tracking, retrieving, releasing, returning, and archiving patient records.
- Monitoring record availability, completeness, custody, and location.
- Protecting confidentiality and controlling authorized access and disclosure.
- Supporting correction, amendment, retention, and disposition processes.
- Preserving discharge summaries, correspondence, scanned documents, consent evidence, and other governed records.

## Physical Folder Lifecycle

The traditional folder is created or retrieved under the approved patient identifier. It receives registration and demographic information, encounter documentation, investigations, reports, prescriptions, procedures, referrals, follow-up records, discharge documentation, correspondence, consent evidence, and supporting attachments. As the patient moves, the folder is requested, tracked, handed over, returned, filed, or archived.

The digital equivalent must preserve the same logical lifecycle. A digital document or event must identify the patient, relevant encounter or service, author or source, timestamp, status, provenance, filing relationship, and access history. Digital records are not complete merely because they exist in a department's system; they must be discoverable through the governed patient record context.

## Legal Record Principles

Medical Records and organizational policy determine which documents form part of the legal medical record, how documents are signed, how corrections are appended, how releases are authorized, and how retention and disposition are controlled. The Patient context supplies identity linkage but does not own Medical Records' legal or custody policy.

No correction may silently overwrite historical evidence. No record may be deleted solely to make the active view simpler. Authorized reviewers must be able to reconstruct the record lifecycle, including creation, access, routing, filing, release, correction, archive, and restoration.

## Digital Transition and Downtime

When paper is used during approved downtime, Medical Records owns the reconciliation process. Temporary records must be linked to the canonical patient identity and MRN or hospital number, filed or scanned according to policy, reconciled against electronic orders and results, and closed with an audit record. Unreconciled paper must be visible as an operational risk rather than disappearing into a local queue.
