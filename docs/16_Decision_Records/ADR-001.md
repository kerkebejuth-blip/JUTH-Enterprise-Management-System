# ADR-001: HOS as a Reusable Enterprise Platform

## Status
Accepted

## Date
2026-07-07

## Context

The Hospital Operating System (HOS) is being established as a long-term enterprise software platform for hospital operations. The project must support more than a single implementation and must remain sustainable as it evolves from a pilot deployment into a broader health system platform.

The initial deployment is the JUTH Digital Management System (JDMS) for Jos University Teaching Hospital, Nigeria. This creates a strategic need to distinguish between:

- the reusable platform, HOS, and
- the first implementation, JDMS.

Without a clear architectural and product boundary, the project risks becoming a one-off hospital application rather than a scalable, reusable platform.

## Problem Statement

How should the project be structured so that it can support:

- a first deployment at JUTH,
- future hospital deployments,
- shared core services,
- configurable domain modules,
- multi-hospital and statewide expansion?

The project needs a foundation that supports reuse, modularity, governance, and long-term evolution.

## Decision

HOS will be implemented as a reusable enterprise platform for hospital operations.

JDMS will be the first deployment of HOS and will serve as the initial reference implementation for the platform.

This decision establishes the following architectural and product principles:

- HOS is the platform.
- JDMS is the first implementation of HOS.
- Core services and shared capabilities should be designed for reuse.
- Hospital-specific workflows should be implemented as configurable modules whenever possible.
- The architecture should support future expansion to additional hospitals and statewide health platforms.

## Alternatives Considered

### 1. Build JDMS as a standalone hospital system only

This option would deliver the immediate hospital solution quickly but would limit reuse and create significant duplication for future deployments.

### 2. Build a generic platform without a concrete initial implementation

This approach could create a theoretically strong platform but would delay practical validation and reduce early stakeholder confidence.

### 3. Build HOS and JDMS as separate products with no shared architectural contract

This would reduce clarity and create long-term integration and maintenance issues.

## Consequences

### Positive Consequences

- The platform can serve multiple hospitals over time.
- Shared services reduce rework and increase consistency.
- The architecture supports modular growth and future configuration.
- JDMS provides a real-world implementation context for validating the platform.

### Negative Consequences

- The project must manage a higher level of abstraction and architectural discipline.
- Some initial work will support future reuse rather than immediate deployment only.
- Platform governance and modular boundaries require stronger coordination.

## Benefits

- Reusability across hospitals and deployment scenarios
- Better maintainability and long-term evolution
- Clear distinction between platform engineering and implementation-specific delivery
- Improved scalability for multi-hospital and statewide expansion
- Stronger foundation for future AI, analytics, and interoperability services

## Risks

- Platform abstraction may introduce complexity early in the project.
- Teams may over-customize the first deployment rather than reuse platform patterns.
- Insufficient governance could weaken the distinction between HOS and JDMS.
- Future implementations may diverge unless architectural standards are enforced.

## Future Considerations

This ADR should guide future decisions related to:

- module architecture and service boundaries
- configuration versus customization trade-offs
- shared infrastructure and platform services
- multi-hospital deployment models
- statewide platform expansion
- AI and analytics service integration

As the platform matures, additional ADRs should be created for significant technical, architectural, and governance decisions.
